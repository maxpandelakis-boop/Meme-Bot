# Other sell rules for the Trencher desks: result (fake money only)

Pre-registered in research/exits/PREREG-EXITS-2026-11-11.md (commit 2e69adbd0, before any variant was run). Run with
`python3 -I research/exits/check_exit_grid.py <results db>` on origin/results 17be2beee. Nothing was re-tuned after the holdout.

## Verdict: no sell rule makes profit. PROFIT fails on both data sets.

Per trade of 8 $ (the desk's 40% of 20 $), FOMO's fees, holdout = newest third of the days:

| data | rule chosen on the older days | holdout per 8 $ | 95% by day | days in profit | video rule D | pick minus D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bot tips (like "Große") | stop -15%, all at 3x, trail 30%, 1 h | -0.51 | -0.84..-0.11 | 1/11 | -0.27 (-1.28..+0.90) | -0.23 (-1.21..+0.63) |
| new launches (like "Frisch") | stop -8%, all at 2x, trail 10% from the entry, 1 h | -1.71 | -1.99..-1.55 | 0/3 | -2.28 (-2.75..-2.03) | +0.57 (+0.27..+0.75) |

- On the older days not one of the 480 variants had an average above 0, in either data set.
- Bot tips: the picks are not better than the video rule on the holdout (LESS BAD fails). With no fee and no slippage the pick is
  at -0.12 per 8 $ (-0.47..+0.30): these coins are about a coin flip, and the costs (about 0.39 per 8 $ round trip, 5%) are the
  loss. A sell rule cannot remove the costs.
- Bot tips, a warning against reading the holdout as a lead: plain holding for 24 h was the worst rule on the older days (-1.96)
  and +0.82 on the holdout (-1.99..+3.67), and 73 of 480 variants were above 0 there. The interval is wide and crosses 0; the
  sign flipped between the two periods. That is noise from a few runners (BLOB), not a rule.
- New launches: LESS BAD passes (+0.57 per 8 $ against the video rule, 3 of 3 days), on 9 days of data and mostly 15-minute
  candles (median 4 one-minute candles per tip). Still -21% per trade. Without costs still -1.31: these coins fall by
  themselves; no fee change would save them.
- The video rule's gap to the best rule is small next to the loss itself. Shorter holding loses less mainly because it is less
  time in a falling market.

## Method check (Maxim: "do we run the wrong tests?")

1. Fees: trencher/sim.js fee() equals FOMO's table; it gives 0.50 on the 25.22 $ buy and 0.95 on the 65.71 $ sale of the two
   screenshots. The fees booked by both live desks, recomputed from their own activity lists, match to the cent (0.6501 and 1.2766).
2. Live trades against the backtest (research/exits/desk_replay.py, branch deskcheck, GeckoTerminal one-minute candles, 11 Nov
   12:44 UTC; 5 closed trades, so an anecdote, not a test):

| desk | coin | live | backtest on candles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Große | NIHAO | stop at 0.89x, -1.17 | stop at 0.88x, -1.28 |
| Große | DacityCoin | stop at 0.91x, -0.62 | stop at 0.90x, -0.64 |
| Frisch | LMEOW | stop at 0.70x, -2.75 | stop at 0.71x, -2.74 |
| Frisch | PirateCoin | stop at 0.64x, -3.91 | stop at 0.74x, -2.98 (entry price 40% apart) |
| Frisch | FOMO | 2.97x within 2 minutes, +4.93 | entry price 3x higher on the candles: stop, -5.31 |

   - Big, liquid coins (240k to 290k $ liquidity): the backtest reproduces the live desk within about 0.1 $ per trade.
   - Fresh coins: the two price sources disagree at the buy. The desk's own log: "$HOLD ... Preis in 3s +208.9%", "$solhao ...
     bei 2k MC · Preis in 3s -76.4%" (GeckoTerminal's price at the pick against DexScreener's 3 s later). The Frisch desk's only
     win (FOMO, +4.93) was booked from an entry price a third of what the candles show for that minute; on the candles it is a
     loss. So the Frisch desk's paper fills cannot be trusted in either direction yet; its live result is not evidence.
   - The -8% stop fills at -30% to -36% on fresh coins (the price moves that far between two looks). A backtest that books a
     stop at its level is too kind there; this study books the close.
3. What these tests cannot see: X / social signals (no free history); the desks' own Scout picks (only hours of live trades;
   the stored paths are the Meme-Bot's tips and listed launches, coins of the same kind, not the same coins); anything faster
   than one minute, and after the first hour only 15-minute candles; DEX is not stored, so no per-DEX breakdown; 35 days (tips)
   and 9 days (launches) are short and one market phase; real fills (failed transactions, MEV) are not modelled and would cost more.
4. What the tests agree on, with different data and different code: DESK-2026-11-11 (840 tips), chartexit (8,424 exits, 1,852
   coins, 5 months), SCOUT-2026-11-11 (all pump.fun trades, 58 days), and this grid. None found a profitable rule after fees.

## What follows (as pre-registered)

- No rule is proposed as profitable. The real-money switch stays off.
- LESS BAD on new launches only: the Frisch paper desk could switch to "stop -8%, sell all at 2x, trail 10% from the entry,
  1 h limit"; Maxim decides. It would lose less, not win.
- Worth fixing first: the Frisch desk should skip a buy when its two price sources differ by more than a set bar, otherwise its
  paper result is noise.
