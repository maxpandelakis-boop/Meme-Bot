# Holdout study, 2026-10-21 (fake money only)

The question (the user, 21 Oct): run the bot on the past with the newest days hidden, and keep only what still works on them.
Sources wanted for every outside claim.

## How it was done

- Every data set was split by time before the rule search. Rules were searched on the older part (discovery) only. Each
  rule was then frozen as a script and run once on the hidden part by a separate agent that had not seen the search.
  Caveat: the radar records and the new-launch trades of the hidden windows had been looked at earlier that day by the
  momentum study (research/MOMENTUM-2026-10-21.md), which also defined the FluxBeam family used below. Only the big test's
  hidden scans (16 to 20 Oct) were truly unseen.
- Big test (every coin the bot scanned, priced 1.7 h later, EUR per 20 ticket after fees, clipped -20..+60): discovery 223
  usable scans, 7 Oct 11:02 to 15 Oct 23:48 UTC; hidden 94 scans, 16 Oct 01:05 to 20 Oct 03:59 UTC.
- Launch radar (first sight, sold at the 60-minute look, EUR per 3 ticket after 5% slippage and paper fees): discovery 14/15
  Oct to 18 Oct 11:59 UTC; hidden 18 Oct 12:01 to 21 Oct 03:55 UTC.
- Paper trades (live rule t1, per EUR staked): hidden young 14 Oct 12:18 to 16 Oct 00:12 (174), pick 17 Oct 12:03 to 20 Oct
  19:27 (27), radar 19 Oct 10:23 to 20 Oct 22:54 (107).
- Every result was also checked without the FluxBeam-like pool family (research/MOMENTUM-2026-10-21.md), without the best
  3 coins, per half, per day and per DEX or mint family.
- Cuts looked at on discovery: big test 75 named rules plus about 130 tertile looks, quintile tables for about 70 factors
  and the liquidity-share bins the 0.20 came from; radar 1,247 subgroup evaluations and 4 single-feature scans; paper trades
  683. With that many looks some false hits on discovery are expected; that is what the hidden days are for.

No rule made money after fees on the hidden days; outside the machine-made FluxBeam family nothing did on discovery either,
but for the pick rule's +0.08 on 19 trades. Only 3.5% of the big test's discovery coin-by-scan rows ended in profit at 1.7 h.

## The one rule that held: the liquidity-share veto

Coins 6 to 24 hours old (the 2h profile's age window), FluxBeam-like pools (liquidity 0.9 to 1.1 times the market cap) left
out, EUR per 20:

- Discovery: kept -2.51 against all -2.71 (+0.19). 95% interval of the gain by scan [+0.03, +0.36], by coin [-0.06, +0.43];
  6 of 9 days positive.
- Hidden days: kept -1.98 (910 rows, 104 coins) against all -2.41 (1,421 rows), +0.43. Positive in both halves (+0.38,
  +0.50) and on all 5 days. Intervals by scan [+0.16, +0.70], by coin [+0.08, +0.76], by day [+0.29, +0.61].
- Counted per coin (each coin's mean over its scans, research/check_liqmc.py's way) the vetoed coins also lost more:
  -4.67 against -4.21 on discovery, -4.49 against -3.15 on the hidden days (coins of at least $100k).
- Hidden days only: it holds for pump.fun and for other mints in both halves, and it beats a plain market-cap floor of $150k
  to $250k. On discovery pump.fun coins reversed in the first half (-0.10) and other mints in the second (-0.15), and a
  $200k or $250k floor did as well or better (-2.47, -2.46 against -2.51).
- Why it can work for pump.fun coins: in a constant-product pool (PumpSwap, Raydium v4) the pool's value grows with the
  square root of the price while the market cap grows with the price, so for them the veto is close to a market-cap floor
  near $180k (a coin still near its graduation level). The effect sat mostly in the other mints, for which no mechanism was
  shown (their liquidity was locked, median 100%).

Limits that travel with it:

- The kept coins still lose about 2 per 20. It is loss avoidance, not a profit rule.
- It does not cut wipeouts. The study first counted "to zero" with the stored gone flag (no price), which misses rugs that
  keep a price (CLAUDIA 0.0049x, RST 0.002x). Counting 98% down or more as zero, the vetoed coins went to zero per coin 15.4%
  against 18.8% on discovery and 14.7% against 11.5% on the hidden days: about as often.
- Among the gate passers, the only coins the veto acts on live, the gain is carried by 3 coins per period (GLITCH, BREAK,
  SDEMONS on the hidden days, CALL on discovery): without them the passers do not do better.
- It changes the bot's top pick in 10 of 208 discovery scans and 13 of 92 hidden ones: -2.29 against -2.28 on discovery,
  -1.86 against -2.02 on the hidden days (SDEMONS alone; the first half reverses). So it misses rules 1 and 3 of the
  research brief at the top pick.
- The 0.20 was chosen from the discovery data, not set in advance. The big test stores no DEX, so the DEX checks used
  proxies; coins on 2 DEXes reversed slightly (-0.18, 145 rows).

Decision: the user asked for a change to the filter after hearing this, so the 2h profile now fails a coin whose liquidity
is 20% of its market cap or more (`GATE_MAX_LIQMC`, why tag `liqmc`), as an exception to the brief's bar. The 24h profile,
the new-launch tab and the radar are unchanged. PAPER_RULE, RP_V and PEAK_V are unchanged.

Forward check, written before its data: `python3 -I research/check_liqmc.py <data dir>` on the big-test export of 4 Nov 2026,
over the scans from 20 Oct 04:00 UTC (right after the hidden days, so no scan of the study). It counts per coin among the
coins the gate without the veto passes. The veto stays only if the vetoed coins average worse per 20 than the kept ones
(at least 20 vetoed coins) and the bot's top pick by score averages at least as well with the veto as without it. Run on
the study's own data it says "stays" on both parts: per coin -5.26 against -3.27 on discovery (whose passers include the
older, wider gate's) and -3.30 against -2.88 on the hidden days; top pick -2.29 against -2.42 and -1.85 against -2.02. The
top pick part rests on 13 to 15 changed picks per period, so it can go either way by chance. The training's gate audit (`gates` in
train.json) is no substitute: it pools 14 days, mixes passers from before the switch-on with those after, and counts rows,
not coins.

## What the outside sources say, and what our data says to them

The web agent could not open pages directly in this environment (DNS failures); every claim below was read from search
excerpts, except MELT, whose repository was cloned and its label file counted. Treat the numbers as the sources report them.

- Most pump.fun tokens die fast: of 18.67M tokens (Jan 2024 to Jun 2026), 68.67% made their last trade on launch day.
  CoinGecko study via press: https://cryptobriefing.com/pump-fun-token-survival-rate-study/ (industry analysis).
  Our data points the same way: 67.0% / 67.6% (discovery / hidden) of pump.fun pools the radar saw were dead 60 minutes
  after first sight. The closeness to 68.67% is a coincidence of definitions, not a replication; the day-1, day-2 and 90-day
  numbers cannot be tested here. Pump.fun coins under 24 h die within 1.7 h about 26 points more often than those over 48 h
  (all coins about 21 to 22); almost all of that is before 6 h of age, which the 2h gate already cuts off.
- Rugs on Solana pools come fast: median about 35 minutes from creation to the first fraud sale, 76,469 of 100,063 new pools
  flagged (H1 2025). arXiv 2603.24625, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24625 (preprint).
  Our data: rugs come fast here too, faster than the paper's median. 76.5% / 75.5% of radar pools rugged within 60 minutes
  (gone, down 80% or liquidity under $1,000), 87% of those by 30 minutes; the rug rate falls from about 7.5% a minute in the
  first 5 minutes to 0.4% a minute after 30. The likeness to the paper's 76.4% flagged is not a replication: definitions and
  windows differ, and 57% of our rugs are liquidity pulls (mostly one Meteora pool family).
- Graduated pump.fun coins mostly lose and coordinated wallets are common: MELT data set, 46,139 migrated launches (Dec 2024
  to Feb 2025), 19.7% with a positive return. https://github.com/git-disl/MELT (paper arXiv 2602.13480, under submission).
  Our data: the loss part holds (only 17.9% / 18.2% of graduated pools end above entry at 60 minutes); the rule "more snipers
  or bundles = worse" fails, sniped coins even did better.
- Fast early liquidity with few trades predicts graduation: arXiv 2602.14860, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14860. Not tested
  (graduation is not profit after graduation).
- Wash trading inflates activity signals (at least 17% of pump.fun trades): arXiv 2609.10246, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10246,
  and arXiv 2507.01963, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01963. Not tested here.
- Early buyer counts are inflated by sniper rings: arXiv 2607.02795, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795 (single author, weak).
  Not tested.
- The first 5 minutes predict rugs within an hour: arXiv 2608.20271, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20271. Not tested.
- Telegram links go with graduation but did not carry over to a later period: arXiv 2607.02823, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02823.
  Not tested.
- Mechanism of the veto: Uniswap v2 constant product, https://app.uniswap.org/whitepaper.pdf; PumpSwap as the constant-product
  pool graduated coins move to, https://www.theblock.co/post/347360/pump-fun-launches-dex-called-pumpswap-to-instantly-migrate-graduated-tokens

## Lessons

- A rule that wins on discovery fails on the hidden days 7 times out of 8 here. Without the hidden days every one of them
  would have looked like an improvement.
- DEX mix fakes edges: the radar's liveness rule only won because the base held Meteora pools, 98% of them dead.
- Count per coin, not per row: the big test lists a coin in every scan, the bot buys it once. The veto's "2 to 3 times as
  often to zero" was rows and the stored gone flag; per coin and with 98% losses counted, it was about as often.
- Check the definition of "to zero" before claiming fewer wipeouts: the stored gone flag misses rugs that keep a price.
